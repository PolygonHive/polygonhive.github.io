    Camera system¶

    The camera is built on Unreal's Gameplay Cameras. A Blueprint camera director picks camera rigs every frame from the character's state, and the component AC_CameraSystem handles the perspective, attaches the camera to the right mesh, and draws the crosshair.
    Folder /GASPALS/Systems/CameraSystem
    Component AC_CameraSystem (on both pawns)
    Camera asset CameraAsset_SandboxCharacter (the pawns' Gameplay Camera component uses it)
    Director CameraDirector_SandboxCharacter (Blueprint camera director)
    Rigs Rigs/ (chooser tables CHT_CameraRig* and camera rigs, one subfolder per perspective)
    Data Data/ (enums E_CameraPerspective, E_CameraStyle, E_CameraMode, E_CameraSide, E_FixedCameraMouseAimType; struct S_CameraOffsetRigs)
    Widget W_Crosshair
    Camera shake CameraShake_Basic (played by the takedown montages' camera shake notify)
    Input IA_Camera_Perspective 1 / gamepad View, IA_Camera_Style 2, IA_Camera_SwitchShoulder Alt / R3
    Settings Camera reference

    Perspectives¶

    Perspective Camera Attached to
    ThirdPerson (default) Over-the-shoulder camera with collision push and damping. The visible mesh's Third Person Camera Bone (root)
    TopDown Fixed-angle camera above the character; the character aims at the mouse cursor or with the right stick. The visible mesh's Third Person Camera Bone (root)
    SideScroller Fixed side view; aims like top-down. The visible mesh's Third Person Camera Bone (root)
    FirstPerson Eye camera on the first-person mesh. See First person. The first-person lead mesh's camera bone (Camera Bone in each body's first-person setup, default the VB Camera virtual bone)

    1 cycles through Allowed Perspectives in their order. By default: third person, top-down, side-scroller, first person, back to third person. Each switch prints the new perspective on screen. The visible mesh is the retargeted character's when one is active, so the camera follows it.

    Choosing the perspectives¶

    Allowed Perspectives lists the views players can use. Set it on the AC_CameraSystem component of each pawn you use. Common setups:

    Game Allowed Perspectives Result
    First person only FirstPerson Starts in first person; 1 does nothing.
    Third person only ThirdPerson No first-person meshes are built; 1 does nothing. You can also delete AC_FirstPersonSystem from the pawn.
    Third and first person ThirdPerson, FirstPerson 1 toggles between them.

    If Camera Perspective (the start view) is not in the list, the camera starts in the first allowed one and shows an on-screen warning. FirstPerson only counts as available when the pawn has an enabled AC_FirstPersonSystem; when it is listed without one, a warning says so and first person is skipped. With only first person allowed, cover's First Person Sticky Cover setting SwitchToThirdPerson acts like Disabled (Cover).

    The ragdoll view (Camera Perspective During Ragdoll, default ThirdPerson) is only used when it is allowed; otherwise the ragdoll keeps the current view. With a first-person-only list that happens silently while the ragdoll view is still at its ThirdPerson default, so restricting the list needs no other change. If you picked another ragdoll view that is not in the list, a warning says so.

    Styles¶

    The style picks a variant per perspective. 2 cycles it (Style_2, Style_3, Style_4, Style_1, then back) and prints the new style on screen; the console variable DDCvar.CameraStyle (0 to 3) sets it too.

    Style Third person Top-down / side-scroller First person
    Style_1 Close CameraRig_TD_1 / CameraRig_SS_1 Same rig in every style
    Style_2 (default) Medium _2
    Style_3 Far _3
    Style_4 Debug view (CameraRig_DebugView, no offset rigs) _4

    How the rig is chosen¶

    Every frame the director:

    1. Reads the pawn's Get_PropertiesForCamera: perspective, style, mode (Free, Strafe or Aim, from the pawn's rotation mode and aim input; a target lock gives Strafe or Aim), shoulder side, stance and movement mode.
    2. Evaluates the chooser table CHT_CameraRig, which branches by perspective into CHT_CameraRigs_ThirdPerson, _FirstPerson, _TopDown or _SideScroller. Third person picks by style and mode: nine rigs CameraRig_TP_<Close|Medium|Far>_<Freecam|Strafe|Aim>; the Aim rigs blend in smoothly. The third-person fallback is CameraRig_TP_Medium_Freecam. First person always uses CameraRig_FP_Base.
    3. Activates the result, plus the First Person Scale Rig in first person when the pawn's first-person system uses the FirstPersonOverlaySplit mode.
    4. In third person (except the debug style Style_4), stacks the offset rigs from Camera Offset Rigs on AC_CameraSystem:
    Entry Default rig When Offset
    Crouch Rig CameraRig_TP_Offset_Crouch Crouched 50 cm down
    Right Shoulder Rig CameraRig_TP_Offset_Right Camera on the right shoulder (the player's choice, or the cover shoulder switch at a wall end) 100 cm to the side
    Cover Low Rig CameraRig_TP_Offset_CoverLow Behind low cover 80 cm up
    Cover Edge Right Rig / Cover Edge Left Rig CameraRig_TP_Offset_CoverEdgeRight / Left At a cover wall end 60 cm to the open side
    First Person Scale Rig CameraRig_FP_Scale First person, FirstPersonOverlaySplit mode First-person scale 0.25 (keeps arms and weapon out of walls)
    In Air Rig CameraRig_TP_Offset_InAir Jumping or falling 30 cm back, 20 cm up
    Sliding Rig CameraRig_TP_Offset_Sliding Sliding (Mover pawn only) 30 cm down
    Traversing Rig CameraRig_TP_Offset_Traversing Vaulting or climbing 40 cm back
    Ragdoll Rig CameraRig_TP_Offset_Ragdoll Ragdolling in third person 60 cm back, 40 cm up

    The four movement-mode rigs (in air, sliding, traversing, ragdoll) need Pass Movement Mode to Camera on. The cover rigs follow the cover system's Enable Cover Camera Offsets and Enable Cover Shoulder Switch settings (Cover).

    Each pawn sets its own rigs on its camera component, so two characters can use different offsets. Point an entry at your own rig to change an offset, or clear it to turn that offset off. The director's own Camera Offset Rigs is only used for a pawn without AC_CameraSystem.

    The chooser table itself is fixed in the director (the engine's Evaluate Chooser node takes the table as a node setting, not an input). To change which main rig is used when, edit CHT_CameraRig and its nested tables, or duplicate them and the director.

    First person needs no crouch offset: the first-person camera follows the head, so it lowers with the head when the character crouches (about 26 cm with the UEFN mannequin).

    The persistent layers CameraRig_Layer_Global and CameraRig_Layer_Base are activated when the director starts.

    The base rigs hold the shared parameters, and the variants override them:

    Base rig Parameters
    ThirdPerson/CameraRig_TP_Base Boom arm offset, camera offset, field of view, forward / lateral / vertical damping, collision push
    TopDown/CameraRig_TopDown_Base, SideScroller/CameraRig_SideScroller_Base Camera translation and rotation, field of view, damping
    FirstPerson/CameraRig_FP_Base Field of view, offset, a First Person node (keeps first-person meshes out of the third-person shadow; do not remove it)

    To change the look, open the rigs in the Camera Rig Asset editor. To change which rig is used when, edit the chooser tables. See Customize the camera.

    Crosshair and aim¶

    For the locally controlled player, AC_CameraSystem creates the Crosshair Widget Class (default W_Crosshair) and keeps it centered on the aim point:

    • Third and first person: a line trace (Aim Trace Channel, default Visibility; Aim Trace Distance, default 100 m) from the camera along its forward vector; the dot sits where it hits, or at the end of the ray.
    • Top-down and side-scroller: the same trace from the mouse cursor into the world, so the dot sits on the point under the cursor; with a gamepad, a point 3 m out along the right stick direction.

    In the fixed cameras the character also turns toward the aim. Fixed Camera Mouse Aim Type picks how the mouse gives that direction: MouseTraceWithPositionFallback (default) faces the world point under the cursor, or uses the cursor's screen position when nothing is under it; MouseScreenPosition always uses the cursor's position relative to the screen center.

    GetCrosshairAim returns the aim origin, direction, point, whether it hit, and a flattened direction. The pawns pass it on through the pawn interface function Get_AimRay (with the trace channel and distance), which is what cover targeting and aim from cover read; aim from cover traces with the same channel and length, so the crosshair and the cover line of fire never disagree.

    The camera component only creates and places the crosshair, at the point the aim ray hits. The widget decides how it looks: W_Crosshair is a dot in its Default Color (white). Cover targets are shown by the cover marker, not the crosshair; a custom crosshair can still ask the cover system (IsCoverTargeted).

    Turn Show Crosshair off to hide the dot (it is collapsed, not destroyed). The aim ray, cover targeting and the cover marker keep working.

    Ragdoll camera¶

    With Change Perspective During Ragdoll on (default), the camera switches to Camera Perspective During Ragdoll (default ThirdPerson) while the character ragdolls, and back to the previous view afterwards. With the defaults, a player in first person, top-down or side-scroller sees the ragdoll in third person; a player already in third person keeps the view, and the Ragdoll Rig offset pulls the camera back. Both pawns trigger it: the CMC pawn through AC_RagdollSystem, the Mover pawn from its Ragdoll movement mode.

    The switch only happens when the ragdoll view is in Allowed Perspectives (see Choosing the perspectives). Only the player's own copy switches; the other machines get the perspective through replication.

    Allow Camera Switch (default off) decides whether the perspective and shoulder inputs work during the ragdoll; looking around is never blocked. If the player changes the perspective during the ragdoll, that view is kept afterwards. Both ragdoll settings only apply while Change Perspective During Ragdoll is on.

    Settings¶

    On AC_CameraSystem (Details panel, Camera category). Both sample pawns use these defaults.

    Setting Category Default Use
    Enable Camera System General true Off: a fixed third-person view with the default style and shoulder; the perspective, style and shoulder inputs do nothing, there is no crosshair, and the ragdoll keeps the view. Read when play starts.
    Third Person Camera Bone General root Bone or socket of the visible mesh the camera attaches to in every perspective but first person.
    Camera Style General Style_2 (medium) Start style.
    Is Camera Right Shoulder General false Start shoulder.
    Camera Perspective Perspective ThirdPerson Start perspective. Must be in Allowed Perspectives.
    Allowed Perspectives Perspective ThirdPerson, TopDown, SideScroller, FirstPerson Views the player can use; 1 cycles them in this order.
    Show Crosshair Crosshair true Show the crosshair dot for the local player.
    Crosshair Widget Class Crosshair W_Crosshair Any User Widget; it is added to the viewport and centered on the aim point.
    Aim Trace Channel Crosshair Visibility Channel of the aim ray (crosshair, cover targeting, aim from cover).
    Aim Trace Distance Crosshair 10000 Length of the aim ray in cm.
    Camera Offset Rigs Rigs the ten rigs above Offset rigs the director stacks on the main rig. Clear an entry to turn that offset off.
    Pass Movement Mode to Camera Rigs true The pawn tells the camera whether it is on the ground, in the air, sliding, traversing or ragdolling, for the movement-mode rigs (and for your own chooser rows). Off = always on the ground. The CMC pawn has no slide.
    Change Perspective During Ragdoll Ragdoll true See Ragdoll camera.
    Camera Perspective During Ragdoll Ragdoll ThirdPerson See Ragdoll camera.
    Allow Camera Switch Ragdoll false See Ragdoll camera.
    Fixed Camera Mouse Aim Type FixedCamera MouseTraceWithPositionFallback Fixed cameras: how the mouse turns the character (see Crosshair and aim).

    Set new values on each pawn

    Change these settings on the AC_CameraSystem component of each pawn Blueprint (select the component in the Components panel). A changed class default does not reach a pawn whose component already overrides that value.

    The Gameplay Cameras setup needs the data-driven console variable DDCVar.NewGameplayCameraSystem.Enable (default true, see Console variables). When it is false or missing, the pawns fall back to a plain spring arm camera and none of this runs.

    Using the camera from Blueprints¶

    Call Use
    SetCameraPerspective Change the perspective to the one passed in (re-attaches the camera, tells the server, fires OnCameraPerspectiveChanged). It does not check Allowed Perspectives: call IsPerspectiveAvailable first.
    OnCameraPerspectiveChanged Event dispatcher with the new perspective.
    RegisterCameraListener(Listener) (BPI_CameraSystem) Adds a component implementing BPI_CameraSystemListener; after every perspective change the camera calls its OnCameraViewChanged(bFirstPerson). AC_FirstPersonSystem registers itself this way.
    RequestFirstPersonView(bFirstPerson) (BPI_CameraSystem) Switch to first person (true) or third person (false); returns false when the camera system is disabled.
    IsFirstPersonView, IsFixedCameraView, IsCameraRightShoulder (BPI_CameraSystem) Current view and shoulder, for other systems.
    IsPerspectiveAvailable(Perspective) True when the perspective is allowed (and, for first person, the pawn has an enabled AC_FirstPersonSystem). With Enable Camera System off, only ThirdPerson.
    GetNextAvailablePerspective, GetFirstAvailablePerspective What 1 would switch to; the fallback start view.
    GetCrosshairAim The aim ray and point. Only valid for player-controlled pawns.
    Get_AimRay (pawn interface) The same ray plus the trace channel and distance; zeros when the pawn has no camera system or no player controller.
    CameraPerspective, CameraStyle, bIsCameraRightShoulder Current state.

    Other systems reach the camera through BPI_CameraSystem (find the pawn's component that implements it), so they keep working when a pawn has no camera system.

    Networking¶

    The perspective is set on the owning client and sent to the server (Server_SetCameraPerspective); it replicates to the other machines. Style, shoulder and crosshair are local to the player. The component needs Component Replicates on; both sample pawns set it on their AC_CameraSystem.

    Limitations¶

    • A new style or mode needs matching chooser rows and rigs.
    • The offset rigs (crouch, shoulder, cover, movement modes) apply in third person only.
    • The main rig chooser table can't be swapped per pawn (see How the rig is chosen).
    • Rig graph edits need the Camera Rig Asset editor; save each rig asset after editing.
